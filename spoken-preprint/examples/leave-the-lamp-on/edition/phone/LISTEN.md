# Leave the Lamp On / 灯先留着

Original fictional scene. The characters and project history are not the listener's real experience.

> 8:11 p.m. A small alterations shop beside a canal. Rain outside. Nell is working on a customer's yellow jacket under a lamp. Idris is coming in backwards carrying a worn wooden armchair. The shop ordinarily closes at 8:30. This is a new fictional encounter; none of its dialogue is predetermined.
> 
> PEOPLE
> Nell: Proprietor of the alterations shop; knows Idris well.
> Idris: A familiar neighbour bringing a wooden armchair.

**[e-db49122200884d9b] PUBLIC OBSERVATION**

> Outside, a neighbouring shutter rattles down. Someone calls farther along the canal, “See you tomorrow!”

**[e-372120303a0d4b28] Nell**

**[e-be00512463fa49ec] Idris**

> [I stop with the chair still lifted.] “There’s a tear in the side I haven’t told my nephew about yet. Could I put it down here and warm up for five minutes? My left shoe’s letting the canal in.”

**[e-f6523c381f5c44a1] Nell**

> “Set it down by the door, clear of the cloth. Five minutes, mind—I’m closing on time tonight. Is it your nephew’s chair?”

**[e-91c1aabac9804a83] Idris**

> [I lower the chair by the door, clear of the cloth, and rub my palms.] “I’ve offered it to him. I had half eight in mind, but I haven’t checked where he is. I might have planned this backwards.”

**[e-39e8e17b7c9443fd] Nell**

> [I rest a hand on the pinned hem and look over at the chair.] “You came in backwards too. Show me the tear.”

**[e-0dbdb8423d0c4e14] Idris**

> [I turn the chair so the torn side faces her.] “Here. That’s the bit he hasn’t seen. Rather spoils the grand gesture, doesn’t it?”

**[e-8a7c980f128740c8] PUBLIC OBSERVATION**

> A low wooden knock sounds as the chair meets the floor.

**[e-6f16a0c4b92c43d7] Nell**

> [I leave the jacket under the lamp and step closer to the torn side.] “Did that happen on the way here, or have you been keeping it turned to the wall?”

**[e-8235735a01604a01] Idris**

> “It was there already. I might’ve chosen a flattering angle.” [I look down at the torn side.] “Is it worth fixing?”

**[e-4f5894f5d1554c23] Nell**

> [I crouch beside the chair and gently pinch the cloth just beyond the tear.] “Maybe. I need to know if the fabric around it will hold a stitch.”

**[e-e1a25deb6c7f4e95] Nell**

> [I let go of the cloth and return to the yellow jacket.] “It’s worn thin there. A patch might do, but I’d ask him if he wants the chair before you pay anyone to mend it.”

**[e-02d54625d6304b21] Idris**

> “I was hoping you’d say it was nothing.” [I take out my phone and crouch to frame the torn side.] “I’ll send him a close-up. I do want him to have it, though.”

**[e-333099d51ca24f33] Nell**

> [I turn the jacket’s hem towards me.] “Why do you want him to have this particular chair?”

**[e-17be16734ab24bde] Idris**

> [I lower the phone.] “He used to sit sideways in it, feet over that arm. I remember finding half a biscuit down the side after he’d gone home. Thought he might like having it.”

**[e-7fe69b6dc564495c] Nell**

> [I bend over the yellow hem again.] “I hope you’ve checked down the side since. Old biscuits are extra.”

**[e-11910177a12d4d9f] Idris**

> “He can have those for nothing, too.” [I photograph the tear, attach it to a message asking if he still wants the chair and whether he’s coming tonight, and tap send.]

**[e-3ddc7a2643f540eb] Idris**

> [I pocket the phone.] “It’s gone through. Have you got an old rag? I’ll get that puddle up.”

**[e-8f1067db139d4858] Nell**

> [I pull a grey cotton rag from beneath the counter and hold it out.] “Here. Thanks for that.”

**[e-a25cf36e75cb43b4] Idris**

> [I take the rag and crouch to wipe the water from beneath the chair.] “Cheers. You off somewhere tonight?”

**[e-4201cb9932f3416f] PUBLIC OBSERVATION**

**[e-e4e087d86dd744a4] Nell**

**[e-ba6f292208544433] Idris**

> [I fold the rag and wipe the glistening strip of floor again.] “Rather you than me in this weather. Have you got a spare carrier bag? I could tie it round this shoe for the walk home.”

> [I take a folded carrier bag from a drawer and lay it on the counter’s edge.] “Here you are. That’s as close as I get to shoe repairs.”
